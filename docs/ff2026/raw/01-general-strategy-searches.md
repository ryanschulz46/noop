## Scoring Structure

Half-PPR scoring is a hybrid scoring format that combines elements of standard and full-point-per-reception (PPR) scoring, where players only receive 0.5 points per reception compared to a full point for each reception in a PPR scoring system.

## Impact on Running Back Value

The key difference is how running backs are valued relative to their receiving volume:

- Rushing yards matter a bit more in half-PPR formats than full ones, so one can make the case for those running backs earlier
- Elite running backs who catch passes are relatively more valuable in half-PPR leagues compared to standard leagues
- Big-play receivers like Mike Evans, Jameson Williams and Alec Pierce are worth more in half-PPR formats, relative to scale, rather than they do in the full-PPR leagues that reward volume receivers

## Concrete Scoring Differences

To illustrate the impact, a running back who catches 8 passes for 60 yards and scores a touchdown earns 16 points from yardage and the score in standard scoring, 24 points in full PPR, and 20 points in half-PPR.

## Draft Strategy Adjustments

When drafting your team in a half-PPR league, prioritize pass-catching running backs: target players who excel in both rushing and receiving to maximize your point potential. However, there isn't such a major difference that it greatly affects strategy overall.
