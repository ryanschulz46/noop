## 5. THE 4-PLAYOFF-SPOT PROBLEM: WHO IS TOO SLOW TO MATTER

**The math.** 4 of 14 = **28.6%** qualify. You must finish top-4 of 14, with points-for as the tiebreaker. That is not a "get hot late" format — it is a "be excellent from Week 1" format. A rookie who is a zero for eight weeks has not merely failed to help; he has occupied one of your four bench spots during the exact stretch that decides whether you are in the field at all.

**The waiver interaction is the part most people miss, and it makes this worse.** Your waiver order **resets weekly by inverse standings, no FAAB**. If you are contending — which is the entire objective — you will sit at waiver priority **11th through 14th every single week**. That means:

- You **cannot** reliably win a contested claim on the Week-10 rookie breakout. The four teams ahead of you in line are the four worst teams, and they will take him.
- So "I'll just grab him off waivers when he breaks out" is **structurally unavailable to a good team in this league.**

That argues *for* some pre-emptive rostering. But it collides head-on with §6: you have four bench spots. The resolution is not "roster more rookies." It is **roster rookies whose value is FRONT-LOADED, not back-loaded** — because a front-loaded rookie helps in the window that decides your season *and* sidesteps the waiver problem entirely.

**This is why Jadarian Price is the single best rookie fit for this specific league.** Charbonnet is on PUP off a playoff ACL tear and "might not return until late in the season" (`01:844`); Price "will likely earn more reps in the **first half of the season**" (`01:852`). His projected usage curve is *inverted* relative to the normal rookie ramp — heavy early, uncertain late. In a format where Weeks 1-9 determine playoff qualification and only 28.6% get in, a front-loaded rookie is worth more than his ADP implies, not less.

## 6. THE 4-BENCH-SPOT PROBLEM: THE RULE

**Do the slot accounting.** 13 picks. K and D/ST consume 2. That leaves 11 real players for 7 non-K/DST starters (QB, RB, RB, WR, WR, TE, FLEX) + 4 bench. Now consider what those 4 bench spots must actually do:

- **Bye weeks.** D/ST and K byes are solved on the wire — unlimited transactions, 1-day waivers. Fine, costs no bench. But per your established format math, **streaming does not work at QB and TE** (14 starters needed; the 15th is bad). So a QB bye and a TE bye either cost you a bench spot each, or cost you a bad week.
- **Injury insurance at RB/WR.** This is what bench spots are actually for.
- **Option value.** With no FAAB and unlimited adds, an empty-ish bench spot is a real asset — but per §5, as a contender you're claiming 11th-14th, so its value is capped to *uncontested* adds.

Realistically you have **1-2 discretionary bench spots**, not 4.

### THE RULE

> **A bench player must beat the best free agent available in the average week, plus the option value of the slot. A rookie who needs six-plus weeks to matter fails that test automatically.**
>
> **Draft rookies who project to START for you: as many as ADP justifies — realistically a maximum of two (Love and/or Price).**
> **Draft speculative rookie bench darts: MAXIMUM ONE. Zero is fully acceptable and is often correct.**
> **Never draft two darts. Never draft a dart before your last two real-player picks (~Rounds 10-11, given K and D/ST).**

**If you take the one dart, take the one whose path is a defined role, not a needed injury.** By the corpus, that is **Jonah Coleman** — Yahoo "soft target," and an explicit stated path: "could be a strong candidate to eventually carve out a **third-down role** thanks to his reliable pass-blocking and capable receiving skills" (`01:790`), with 54 college receptions (`03`). In half-PPR with a FLEX, a receiving-down back has a weekly floor. Kaytron Allen is the alternative on situation ("one of the more open situations for running backs in the NFL," `01:1285`) but the same sentence calls him a *dynasty* stash — which is precisely the wrong asset for a 28.6%-playoff redraft league.

**One tactical note, flagged as NOT corpus-supported:** you have 1 IR slot. If a rookie carries an ESPN IR-eligible designation on draft day, he costs zero bench spots, which changes his price entirely. The corpus says nothing about any 2026 rookie's IR eligibility — check the draft room live. This would matter most for Kaelon Black (injured, `02:1207`) and is the only scenario in which I would take a second rookie dart.
