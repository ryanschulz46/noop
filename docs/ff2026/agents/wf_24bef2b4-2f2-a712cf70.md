## 4. How 0.5 PPR Changes WR Ranking Order vs Full PPR

### 4.1 The exact mechanic

The only difference between full PPR and this league is **0.5 × receptions**. Everything else (0.1/yd, 6-pt receiving TD) is identical. So the full-PPR→half-PPR delta for any receiver is exactly:

```
Δ = −0.5 × (season receptions)
```

That's it. A 115-catch receiver loses 57.5 points vs full PPR. A 60-catch receiver loses 30. **The entire half-PPR adjustment between those two archetypes is 27.5 points, or ~1.6 PPG.** This is real but bounded — it moves players a few slots within a tier, it does not vault a WR3 into the WR1 tier.

Two conversion facts worth memorizing at the draft table:

| Currency | Full PPR | **Half PPR (this league)** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 reception is worth… | 10 receiving yards | **5 receiving yards** |
| 1 receiving TD is worth… | 6 receptions | **12 receptions** |
| 1 receiving TD is worth… | 60 rec yards | 60 rec yards (unchanged) |

**The headline: relative to receptions, touchdowns are exactly twice as valuable in this league as in full PPR.** Yards-to-TD weighting is unchanged; catches-to-TD weighting doubles. That single ratio drives every conclusion below.

### 4.2 Worked archetype models (17 games)

| Archetype | Stat line | **Half-PPR** | Full PPR | Standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Volume slot / possession** | 145 tgt, 115 rec, 1,150 yds, 5 TD | **202.5** | 260.0 | 145.0 |
| **B — Field-stretcher / TD hog** | 105 tgt, 65 rec, 1,150 yds, 11 TD | **213.5** | 246.0 | 181.0 |
| **C — True alpha X** | 155 tgt, 100 rec, 1,450 yds, 9 TD | **249.0** | 299.0 | 199.0 |
| **D — Bellcow RB (for FLEX comparison)** | 250 car / 1,100 rush, 45 rec / 350 rec, 10 TD | **227.5** | 250.0 | 205.0 |

Read the flips:

- **A vs B:** In full PPR the slot WR wins by **+14**. In half PPR the field-stretcher wins by **+11**. A **25-point swing**, and a genuine order flip. If your board is a full-PPR board, you are systematically over-ranking Archetype A.
- **D vs A:** In full PPR the RB trails the volume WR by **−10**. In half PPR the RB leads by **+25**. A **35-point swing.** Half-PPR is a meaningful thumb on the scale toward RBs in the FLEX — and this league already forces RB2.
- **C is untouched in rank.** True alphas own *both* volume and TDs, so they lose the most raw points (−50) but lose nothing relative to their peers. Half-PPR does not de-throne elite WRs; it only reshuffles the middle.

### 4.3 The break-even efficiency threshold (the precise answer)

Points per target, half-PPR, ignoring TDs:
```
PPT = catch_rate × (0.5 + 0.1 × YPR)
```

Take a prototypical high-volume slot: **75% catch rate, 10.0 YPR** → `0.75 × (0.5 + 1.0) = 1.125 pts/target`.

What YPR must a **60% catch rate** vertical WR post to match him on equal targets?

| Format | Equation | Required YPR |
|---|---|---|
| Full PPR | `0.60 × (1.0 + 0.1y) = 1.50` | **15.0** |
| **Half PPR** | `0.60 × (0.5 + 0.1y) = 1.125` | **13.75** |

**Half-PPR lowers the bar for a low-catch-rate deep threat by exactly 1.25 yards per reception.** Equivalently, the efficiency premium on aDOT rises about **9%** (deep/slot PPT ratio goes from 1.105 in full PPR to 1.20 here).

### 4.4 Who LOSES value in this format

Rank these down 3–8 spots off a full-PPR board:

1. **Pure low-aDOT slot receivers** — the 6.5–9.0 YPR, 110+ catch, sub-6-TD profile. This is the single most over-drafted archetype in half-PPR. Their entire edge is the currency that just got halved.
2. **Screen-and-manufactured-touch WRs** — jet sweeps, bubbles, WR-as-RB usage. High reception count, low YPR, low red-zone role.
3. **WR2s on run-heavy offenses who survive on checkdown volume** — they need catches to be viable and have no TD equity to fall back on.
4. **Small-bodied WRs with no red-zone role.** A receiver who is schemed out of the 10-yard line caps his own TD ceiling, and TDs are the currency that just doubled in relative worth.
5. **Ancillary/target-hog TEs and pass-catching RBs competing for your FLEX** — same halving, and they have even less yardage and TD equity to compensate.

### 4.5 Who GAINS value in this format

Rank these up 3–8 spots:

1. **Contested-catch red-zone X receivers.** 6'2"+ boundary WRs with a designated goal-line role. TD:reception weighting doubled; this is their format.
2. **Vertical Z / field-stretchers with 15+ YPR.** The break-even math above (13.75 YPR) means they clear the bar much more easily here.
3. **Any WR on a top-5 scoring offense**, because TD equity is the leveraged variable. Offense quality matters more in half-PPR than in full PPR.
4. **"Big slot" 15+ YPR types** — they get the slot's target volume *and* clear the efficiency threshold.
5. **Boom/bust WRs generally.** ⚠️ Important caveat: this cuts both ways. Boom/bust WRs gain in *season-long total*, but in a league that starts only **WR2 + FLEX** and has a **4-team playoff with 2-week matchups**, week-to-week floor still has independent value. See §6.6.

### 4.6 Second-order: 4-pt passing TDs magnify the receiver

Passing TD = 4, receiving TD = 6. A 40-yard TD pass produces:
- QB: `40 × 0.04 + 4 = 5.6`
- WR: `40 × 0.1 + 6 = 10.0`

**The receiver banks 1.8× what the quarterback does on the identical play.** Combined with `−2` per INT, QB scoring in this league is heavily compressed. Concretely: a 4,500-yd / 32-TD / 12-INT / 350-rush-yd / 3-rush-TD QB scores ~337 (19.8 PPG); a replacement 4,000-yd / 25-TD / 12-INT QB scores ~252 (14.8 PPG). **A 5 PPG spread that waiver wire QBs routinely close.** This is a strong "wait on QB, spend the picks on RB/WR" league. (Rushing QBs get an extra boost: rush TD = 6 vs pass TD = 4.)

## 6. Does WR2 + 1 FLEX Suppress WR Value? Yes — and here is exactly how much.

### 6.1 Starting-slot demand (the supply/demand math)

Assume FLEX usage splits ~55% WR / 40% RB / 5% TE in a half-PPR, 2-RB-required league.

| League | Required WR slots | FLEX WR slots | **Total WR starting slots** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10-team, this league (2WR)** | 20 | 5.5 | **25.5** |
| 10-team, 3WR comparison | 30 | 5.5 | 35.5 |
| **12-team, this league (2WR)** | 24 | 6.6 | **30.6** |
| 12-team, 3WR comparison | 36 | 6.6 | 42.6 |

**Answer to "by how much": exactly a 28.2% reduction in WR starting demand** (25.5/35.5 = 30.6/42.6 = 0.718), and the figure is identical at both league sizes.

Meanwhile RB demand is *unchanged* at ~24 slots (10-tm) / ~28.8 (12-tm).

**The WR:RB starting-slot ratio collapses from 1.48 (3WR league) to 1.06 (this league).** That is the whole story in one number: **this is structurally an RB-leaning league by 2026 standards**, and half-PPR (§4.2, the +35 swing) pushes the same direction.

### 6.2 Replacement level moves — and the hit is NOT uniform in relative terms

| League | Weekly replacement-level WR |
|---|---|
| 12-team, 3WR | ~WR43 |
| **12-team, this league** | **~WR31** |
| 10-team, 3WR | ~WR36 |
| **10-team, this league** | **~WR26** |

Replacement level rises by **10–12 ranking spots**. On the flat part of the WR curve that's roughly **0.8–1.2 PPG, or ~15–20 half-PPR points over a season** *(derived estimate from typical curve shape — not a verified 2026 figure)*.

Because VOR subtracts a **constant** from every WR, the *relative* damage is wildly uneven:

- An **alpha** carrying ~90 points of VOR loses ~18 → **−20% of his edge.**
- A **WR3-type** carrying ~40 points of VOR loses ~18 → **−45% of his edge.**
- A **WR5-type** carrying ~15 points of VOR loses ~18 → **his edge goes to zero or negative.**

> ### 🎯 The core strategic conclusion
> **Elite WRs keep ~80% of their value. Mid and late WRs lose 45–100% of theirs.**
> Draft the alphas with total confidence — but **never spend a pick on a WR purely for depth.** In this format, WR depth is worth approximately nothing.

### 6.3 The bench constraint is the real story (and it dwarfs the 2WR effect)

**4 bench spots. 13 active roster slots.** This, not the 2WR requirement, is the defining feature of the league.

A realistic 13-man roster:
```
QB 1 | RB 4–5 | WR 4–5 | TE 1 | D/ST 1 | K 1  = 13
```

**You will draft only 4–6 WRs total, period.**

Draft-pool consequences:
- 12-team: ~156 players drafted. Subtract ~48 for QB/TE/D-ST/K → ~108 RB+WR → **~54 WRs drafted; WR54 is roughly the last one off the board.**
- 10-team: ~130 drafted → ~90 RB+WR → **~45 WRs drafted.**

Compare a 12-team / 16-roster / 3WR league where ~65 WRs go. The *draft* pool is only modestly shallower — but the **in-season rostered** pool is dramatically shallower, because every one of your 10–12 opponents is equally spot-starved and cannot hoard. (The WR-8 position max is irrelevant; the 13-slot ceiling binds first.)

### 6.4 Waiver rules make WR depth nearly free — exploit this hard

The transaction settings are unusually permissive:
- **No limit on season acquisitions**
- **NOT FAAB** — no budget to deplete
- **1-day waiver period** (fast)
- **Waiver order RESETS WEEKLY by inverse standings** — losing teams get first crack every single week

Combined with a shallow rostered pool, **the WR4/WR5 slot is a rental, not an asset.** Every week there will be startable WRs on waivers, and acquiring them costs nothing but a roster spot.

> **Roster spots — not talent — are the scarcest resource in this league.**

Direct corollaries:
- ❌ **Do not draft handcuff RBs or lottery-ticket stashes.** Each costs 25% of your bench. With a 1-day waiver and unlimited adds, you can claim the backup the moment the starter goes down, at a cost of one weekly waiver priority.
- ❌ **Do not draft a QB2, TE2, K2, or D/ST2.** Stream all bye weeks off waivers for free. (Position maxes of QB 4 / TE 3 / K 3 / D-ST 3 are traps — ignore them entirely.)
- ✅ **All 4 bench spots go to RB/WR who could plausibly start this week or break out soon.**
- ✅ **Draft K and D/ST in the final two rounds only.**

⚠️ One counterweight on D/ST: this league scores **yards allowed** in addition to points allowed, and the penalties are severe (400–449 = **−3**, 450–499 = **−5**, 500–549 = **−6**, 550+ = **−7**). That makes D/ST far more volatile and elite defenses genuinely more valuable than in a normal league. It is still not worth a mid-round pick given 4 bench spots — but it does mean streaming D/ST should be driven by **opponent total yards allowed**, not just projected points allowed. This is the single most unusual rule in the entire league.

### 6.5 Bye weeks

NFL byes run ~weeks 5–14. With 9 starters and only 4 bench spots you **cannot** pre-draft bye coverage. Don't try — the unlimited-acquisition, 1-day waiver system solves it week by week. Just avoid drafting your QB, TE, and both RBs on the same bye, and let the rest go.

### 6.6 Playoff structure changes what "value" means — Week 18 is the sleeper issue

- **Only 4 teams make the playoffs** (40% in a 10-team, **33% in a 12-team**). That is a narrow gate. It argues for **ceiling over floor** during the regular season — you must finish top-4, and a safe 8-6 team misses.
- **Seeding tiebreaker = total points for.** Another push toward ceiling: start your highest-projected lineup, not the safe one, even in games you're likely to win.
- **Playoff matchups are 2 WEEKS LONG.** This halves variance in the playoffs, which **favors true underlying talent over matchup streaming** once you're in. The boom/bust WRs you drafted for their half-PPR TD equity (§4.5) become *safer* here, because two weeks of samples smooth them out. Boom/bust is penalized in the 1-week regular season and rewarded in the 2-week playoffs.
- ⚠️ **A 4-team bracket with 2-week rounds needs 4 weeks.** That most likely means a **Week 1–14 regular season and Weeks 15–18 playoffs.** If so, **your championship includes Week 18 — the week playoff-clinched NFL teams rest starters.** Verify this in the league settings before the draft. If confirmed, it is a genuine tiebreaker on close picks: prefer WRs on teams projected to still be fighting for seeding in Week 18, and downgrade WRs on projected runaway #1 seeds.

### 6.7 Draft-plan summary

**Target roster shape:** `QB 1 | RB 4–5 | WR 4–5 | TE 1 | D/ST 1 | K 1`

| Round (12-tm, 13 rds) | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Best available by **tier**, but RB-first is more defensible here than in a 3WR league (§6.1 + §4.2). Take an alpha WR if a tier break says so. |
| 3–6 | Fill RB2 / WR1 / WR2. This is where you must not drift into WR depth. |
| 7–9 | TE1 if you punted; RB3/RB4 and WR3/WR4 with real weekly-starter paths. |
| 10–11 | QB1 (§4.6 — scoring is compressed, wait). Last upside RB/WR. |
| 12–13 | **D/ST and K. Never earlier.** |

- **Draft order is randomized 1 hour before, 90 sec/pick.** Do not build a rigid round-by-round script. Build a **tiered board with hard tier breaks** and a one-line rule: *take the last player in a tier before it breaks, at the position where the break is steepest.*
- **10-team vs 12-team:** in a **10-team**, replacement level is so shallow (WR26, and free weekly waivers) that WR depth is worth even less — lean harder into elite RB/WR at the top and stream more aggressively. In a **12-team**, WR31 replacement is thinner, so your WR3 matters slightly more, but the conclusion is unchanged in direction.
