## 3. THE RUSHING-QB PREMIUM — QUANTIFIED

### 3a. The exchange rates in your league

- 1 rushing yard = **0.1 pt** = **2.5 passing yards** (0.04)
- 1 rushing TD = **6 pts** = **1.5 passing TDs** (4)
- Therefore: **500 rush yards + 8 rush TDs = 50 + 48 = 98 points**, which is exactly equivalent to **2,450 passing yards** or **24.5 passing TDs**.

That is not a rounding error. A "modest" rushing season for a mobile QB is worth an entire second-tier QB's passing season.

## 5. WITH ONLY 4 BENCH SPOTS — IS 2 QB VIABLE?

### 5a. The roster arithmetic

13 active spots. Mandatory: QB1, RB2, WR2, TE1, FLEX1, D/ST1, K1 = 9. **That leaves exactly 4 bench spots.** A K and a D/ST occupy two starting slots you cannot avoid, so your entire "depth" is 4 players.

Realistic optimal allocation of those 4: **3 RB/WR + 1 swing (the best available upside RB/WR, or a QB2 only in the specific cases below).** In a 12-team league that means only ~156 players are rostered league-wide out of a pool of every NFL player — the waiver wire is bottomless, and **waivers here are 1-day, NOT FAAB, unlimited acquisitions, order resets weekly by inverse standings.** Adding a player costs literally nothing but a roster spot for 24 hours.

### 5c. The answer

**Optimal number of QBs to roster: ONE, for the vast majority of the season. Draft exactly one QB.**

Rationale, all of it specific to this league:
1. Your bench is 4 deep and your FLEX is RB/WR/TE — a 4th RB or 5th WR wins you more weeks than a QB2 ever will (RB VOR at rank 14 = +3.16 vs QB VOR at rank 14 = −0.68 in a 12-team).
2. Waivers are **1-day, free, and unlimited**. A bye-week or injury fill is a two-click operation costing you nothing. That converts the "solo, eat a zero" column (16.5–20.2) into the "solo + free waiver fill" column (17.2–21.6) — you recover ~0.7–3.6 PPG at **zero roster cost**.
3. The extra value of even a *perfect* QB2 over that free fill is only +1.3 to +3.9 PPG, and real-world platooning captures approximately none of it.
4. **You have an IR slot.** Use it for an injured RB/WR, not a QB.

**The three narrow exceptions where a QB2 is correct:**
- **You drafted Lamar Jackson.** He is the one case where the free-waiver-fill line (15.44) *beat* the perfect-QB2 line (15.02) — because his problem was availability, not matchup. Even so, do NOT roster a QB2 all year; just be ready to stream in weeks 12–13 (his bye is W13).
- **You drafted Joe Burrow (8 games in 2025) or Patrick Mahomes (14 games in 2025, W5 bye).** Both have real availability risk. Carry a QB2 only during the specific bye/injury window.
- **Weeks where your QB is on bye.** Pick one up on Tuesday, drop him on Tuesday. Do not carry him.

**Practical rule: draft 1 QB. Add a second only in the week you need him. Never carry two QBs into a regular-season week where both are healthy and playing.** The one late-round exception worth a roster spot is a *stash with starting upside*, and in 2026 that is **Fernando Mendoza (LV)** — the No. 1 overall pick, ECR QB30, listed behind Kirk Cousins, but already **25.9% rostered** (vs Cousins' 7.3%), meaning the market expects him to take the job. That is a lottery ticket, not a QB2.
