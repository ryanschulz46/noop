## PART 2 — THE ELITE-TE EDGE RECOMPUTED AT TE15/16 REPLACEMENT

### 2b. Re-baselining from TE12/13 to TE15/16

A standard 12-team VORP model baselines at ~TE12–13. Our league baselines at **TE15–16**. Extending down through the flat band at <3.0 pts/slot:

```
Elite VORP at 12-team baseline (TE12/13)      >  50.0 half-PPR pts
Deepen baseline ~3 slots to TE15/16
  3 slots x ~2.5 pts/slot                     ≈  + 7.5 pts
------------------------------------------------------------
Elite TE edge at 14-TEAM replacement          ≈  57 half-PPR pts
                                              ≈  3.35 PPG over 17 games
```

**The 14-team format adds only ~7 points to the elite-TE edge.** That is the crux, and it cuts *against* paying up — see 2d.

### 2c. The half-PPR haircut — done correctly

The brief frames it as "half-PPR costs an 85-catch TE 42.5 pts (~2.5 PPG)." That arithmetic is right (85 × 0.5 = 42.5) but it is the **absolute** haircut, and **every TE takes the same per-catch haircut**. What actually moves the draft decision is the **reception differential** between the elite TE and his replacement.

```
McBride 2025 receptions:                   126   (CONFIRMED, 5 blocks)
Est. TE15/16 streamer receptions:          ~50   (UNVERIFIED — corpus gives no
                                                  reception count for any TE15+)
Reception differential:                      76
------------------------------------------------------------
Value of that gap in FULL PPR:   76 x 1.0 =  76.0 pts
Value of that gap in HALF PPR:   76 x 0.5 =  38.0 pts
EDGE DESTROYED BY HALF-PPR:                  38.0 pts
```

Therefore:

```
Elite TE edge, HALF PPR (this league):   ~57 pts  =  3.35 PPG
Elite TE edge, FULL PPR (equivalent):  57 + 38
                                       =  ~95 pts  =  5.59 PPG
------------------------------------------------------------
HALF-PPR CUTS THE ELITE-TE ADVANTAGE BY ~40%
```

**Does the tier still separate after the haircut? Yes — but by 3.35 PPG, not 5.6 PPG.** That distinction is the entire decision. Essentially all "pay up for the elite TE" content is written in a full-PPR frame where the edge is ~95 points and looks overwhelming. In our format it is ~57 points and merely good.

### 2d. The 14-team twist that most people get backwards

The instinct is *"14 teams = more scarcity = pay up for the elite TE."* **The math says the opposite.** Compare how far replacement level moves at each position going from 12 to 14 teams (per the brief's given format math):

| Pos | 12-tm repl. | 14-tm repl. | Slots deeper | Curve steepness in that region |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RB | RB31 | **RB36** | **5** | steep |
| WR | WR29 | **WR34** | **5** | steep |
| TE | TE13 | **TE15/16** | **2–3** | **<3.0 pts/slot — flattest position on the board** |

**TE gains the least from going to 14 teams: fewest slots deeper, across the flattest part of any positional curve.** RB and WR replacement each drop 5 slots through steep terrain. So expanding from 12 to 14 teams **increases the relative value of elite RB/WR versus elite TE.** The single-slot-scarcity intuition is real for D/ST and K (32-team supply) but does not hold for TE, where the corpus shows the TE4–TE14 band is a plateau.

### 4b. The winning kicker profile (scoring-rule analysis, not a player claim)

This is derivable from the league's stated scoring alone. Expected value per attempt, where a miss costs −1:

| Attempt | Accuracy | EV calculation | EV |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30–39 yd | 95% | 0.95(3) + 0.05(−1) | **+2.80** |
| 40–49 yd | 82% | 0.82(4) + 0.18(−1) | **+3.10** |
| **50–59 yd** | **70%** | 0.70(5) + 0.30(−1) | **+3.20** |
| 50–59 yd | 63.3% | 0.633(5) + 0.367(−1) | **+2.80** ← breakeven vs a chip shot |
| 60+ yd | 40% | 0.40(5) + 0.60(−1) | **+1.40** |

**Four conclusions:**

1. **Leg strength pays, and the miss penalty is too small to deter it.** A 50-yarder at just 70% (+3.20) outscores a 33-yarder at 95% (+2.80). The −1 miss penalty is trivial against a +5 make. **A kicker needs only ~63% from 50+ for long attempts to beat chip shots.**
2. **The 60+ bracket is a trap — and this is the quirk worth naming.** 60+ pays **5**, identical to 50–59. So an extreme-range attempt carries **the same reward at roughly half the conversion rate**. EV collapses from +3.20 to ~+1.40. **A kicker who is regularly sent out from 60+ is actively hurting you** relative to one taking the same volume from 50–55. Do not chase "biggest leg in the league" — chase "most attempts in the 45–55 window."
3. **You want an offense that STALLS in field-goal range.** A PAT is worth **1**. A drive that stalls at the 32 instead of scoring converts that 1 point into **4**. So the ideal offense moves the ball between the 20s competently but is **poor in the red zone** — good-not-great. An elite red-zone offense actively starves its kicker.
4. **Dome / warm home matters most in Weeks 15–17**, when your two-week playoff matchups land. Cold, wind, and precipitation compress effective range and raise miss rates exactly when a single −1 inside a two-week aggregate is hardest to recover.

**Composite target profile:** strong-legged, accurate kicker, **dome or warm-weather home**, on a **mid-tier offense with red-zone problems**, with heavy 40–55 yard attempt volume and **no habit of 60+ heaves.** Match that profile against ESPN's live kicker list at draft time.

## PART 5 — D/ST: THE NEGATIVE TAIL, QUANTIFIED

### 5a. First, the correction the brief asks me to lead with

**The yards-allowed brackets (+5 to −7) alongside points-allowed are ESPN's DEFAULT D/ST scoring. This is NOT a league quirk.** Every published D/ST ranking, projection, and ADP already prices yardage in. **Do not re-rank defenses to "account for" yardage, and do not apply any premium or discount on top of consensus D/ST rankings.** Doing so double-counts a rule the market has already absorbed.

### 5b. What IS genuinely true: the tail is real and it is asymmetric

I ran the exact bracket table across realistic game scripts:

**TOP-TIER D/ST**
| Week type | PA | Yds | Plays | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ceiling | 3 (+4) | 180 (+3) | 5 sk, 2 INT, 1 FR, 1 TD | **+24** |
| Strong | 10 (+3) | 250 (+2) | 4 sk, 1 INT, 1 FR | **+13** |
| **Median** | 17 (+1) | 310 (0) | 3 sk, 1 INT | **+6** |
| Poor (blown out) | 31 (−1) | 420 (−3) | 2 sk | **−2** |

**BOTTOM-TIER D/ST**
| Week type | PA | Yds | Plays | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ceiling (turnover luck) | 13 (+3) | 290 (+2) | 3 sk, 2 INT, 1 FR, 1 TD | **+20** |
| **Median** | 27 (0) | 380 (−1) | 2 sk, 1 INT | **+3** |
| Bad | 38 (−3) | 470 (−5) | 2 sk | **−6** |
| **Disaster** | 48 (−5) | 560 (−7) | 1 sk | **−11** |

**Theoretical extremes: floor −12 (52 pts / 600 yds, no plays); ceiling ~+34 (shutout, 150 yds, 6 sk, 3 INT, 2 TD).**

**The story is the floor, not the ceiling.** Both tiers reach roughly +20 on a turnover-luck week — **big D/ST weeks are driven by defensive TDs and takeaways, which are near-random and available to any unit.** The tiers separate on the downside:

```
Top-tier floor:      ~ -2
Bottom-tier floor:   ~ -11
--------------------------------
FLOOR GAP:           ~  9 points
Median gap:          ~  3 points  (+6 vs +3)
```

**A bad D/ST does not post a low week — it posts a genuinely negative one.** A −11 is not "a zero"; it actively erases a starting RB's output from your team total. Contrast the streaming downside at the two positions:

| | Bad-week downside | Realistic worst case |
|---|---|---|
| **Kicker** | ~0 to +2 | ~−2 (two misses, no makes) |
| **D/ST** | −6 to −11 | −12 |

**Streaming a K risks ~2 points. Streaming a D/ST risks ~11.** That is a ~5x difference in blast radius, and it is the real, non-quirk consequence of the negative brackets.

**Two-week playoff matchups amplify this specifically.** Over two weeks variance in *positive* outcomes partially cancels — but a single −11 inside a two-week aggregate is close to unrecoverable, because you cannot offset it with a correspondingly large positive (the elite median is only +6). And with **4 of 14 teams qualifying (28.6%) on a points-for tiebreaker**, negative weeks damage you twice: once in the matchup, once in the season-long PF that decides your seed.

## PART 6 — D/ST: STREAM OR SECURE? Recommendation: SECURE ONE AT ROUND 12

**Recommendation: draft ONE D/ST you are content to start most weeks at Round 12 (picks 155–168). Do not build a stream-from-Week-1 plan, and do not reach before Round 11.**

The reasoning is **structural and turns on the 4-bench constraint, not on the scoring brackets:**

**1. The shallow bench makes streaming MORE expensive, not less.** This inverts the usual advice. Published D/ST streaming strategy assumes an ESPN-default **7-bench** roster, where you carry two defenses and rotate by matchup at near-zero cost. **With 4 bench spots you cannot do that** — a second D/ST consumes 25% of your bench for a position whose median-tier gap is ~3 PPG. So the classic streaming plan is unavailable in its usual form.

**2. Supply is fine; roster space is the binding constraint.**
```
D/ST rostered:  14 of 32 NFL teams
On waivers:     ~18 D/ST, permanently
```
You can *always* find a replacement. What you cannot do is *hold* one in advance. So the correct pattern is **drop-and-re-add**, not roster-two — which works here because waivers are **unlimited, 1-day, and non-FAAB**, with order **resetting weekly by inverse standings**. Acquisitions are free; only roster slots are scarce.

**3. But do not reach.** The median gap between an elite and a mid D/ST is only ~3 PPG. Spending R9 or R10 on a defense costs a real player you cannot replace at RB36/WR34 replacement level. **The negative tail argues for owning a competent unit, not for paying a premium price for the best one.**

**The operating plan:**
- **R12: take the best available D/ST.** Reach to **R11** *only* if a clearly consensus-elite unit is still there and a D/ST run has already started.
- **Never R10 or earlier.**
- **Start it every week by default.** Do not chase weekly matchups — the median gap (~3 pts) is smaller than your error bars, and every swap burns a roster move you may need at RB/WR.
- **Deviate only on (a) bye weeks and (b) genuinely brutal spots** — road games against elite offenses, which is where the −6 to −11 tail actually lives. Those are the only two cases where the tail justifies the roster churn.
- **Use inverse-standings waiver order deliberately:** if you are losing, your priority is high — that is exactly when to grab the best available defense for your playoff push.
