# What I *can* deliver: format analysis from your verified league settings

## 1. The defining constraint: a 4-man bench

9 starters + 4 bench = 13 active. You are **required** to roster a D/ST and a K. That leaves **11 of 13 spots** for QB/RB/WR/TE, and only **4 spots total** of flexibility.

Consequences, in order of importance:

- **Never draft a backup K or backup D/ST.** The position maximums (K 3, D/ST 3) are a trap in a 13-man format. A second K costs you 25% of your bench.
- **Do not roster two QBs** unless the second is a genuine league-winner. In a 1-QB league with a 4-point passing TD, replacement-level QB is very high.
- **Handcuffing is prohibitively expensive.** One handcuff = 25% of your bench. Only handcuff a true bellcow whose backup would immediately be a top-15 RB, and only late.
- **Classic Zero-RB is structurally broken here.** Zero-RB depends on stockpiling six-plus lottery-ticket RBs. You cannot hold six. Secure at least two RBs you can start without flinching.
- Expected final roster shape: **1 QB, 4–5 RB, 4–5 WR, 1 TE, 1 D/ST, 1 K.**

## 2. The waiver system makes streaming nearly free — with one catch

- Waiver period is **1 day**, there is **no acquisition limit**, and it is **not FAAB**. Churn is cheap and constant. This strongly reinforces "draft starters, stream the rest."
- **The catch: waiver order resets weekly by inverse standings.** If your team is good, you will hold a *bad* waiver position all season. You cannot rely on winning contested claims. Plan your D/ST and K streams around players likely to clear waivers, not the obvious hot pickup everyone wants.
- **Lineup locks individually at each player's gametime.** You can play the Thursday/Sunday-early/Sunday-late/Monday sequence, swapping in reaction to inactives. This adds real value to rostering players in later windows.

## 3. D/ST is dramatically more important than in a normal league

This is the single biggest edge available in your format, and most of your leaguemates will miss it.

Your league scores **yards allowed** in addition to points allowed:

| Yards allowed | Points |
|---|---|
| <100 | +5 |
| 100–199 | +3 |
| 200–299 | +2 |
| 300–349 | **0** |
| 350–399 | −1 |
| 400–449 | −3 |
| 450–499 | −5 |
| 500–549 | −6 |
| 550+ | −7 |

League-average NFL yards allowed sits in the low-to-mid 300s per game — squarely in the **0 to −1** band. So the *average* defense earns nothing from the yardage category, while a bad matchup stacks a yardage penalty on top of a points-allowed penalty.

**Worst case is brutal:** 450 yards and 30 points allowed = −5 (yards) + −1 (points) = −6 before any sacks or turnovers claw it back. A genuine blowout can approach −12. That is a lost week from a single roster slot.

Practical implications:

- **Do not punt D/ST.** In most formats D/ST is noise; here the downside tail is large enough to decide matchups.
- **Value yardage-suppressing defenses over turnover-hunting defenses.** Boom-bust takeaway defenses that surrender yardage will bleed points in this system. Bend-but-don't-break defenses are actively penalized.
- **Stream aggressively and by matchup**, targeting opponents with weak, low-volume offenses. Avoid starting any defense facing a high-tempo, high-yardage offense regardless of that defense's reputation.
- Take a strong D/ST a round or two earlier than your instinct says — but still late, and never at the cost of a starting skill player.

## 4. Kicker scoring is distance-weighted with a miss penalty

PAT 1 · FG 0–39 = 3 · 40–49 = 4 · 50–59 = 5 · **60+ = 5 (no additional bonus)** · **missed FG = −1**

- Favor kickers on offenses that generate volume but stall in the 40–55 yard range, and kickers in domes or favorable climates.
- The −1 miss penalty makes low-accuracy big-leg kickers less attractive than raw distance scoring suggests. Note that 60+ attempts are scored the same as 50–59 while carrying a much higher miss rate — a 60-yard attempt is, in expectation, a *negative* for you relative to a 50-yarder.
- **Always draft your kicker with your final pick.** Never earlier. Stream via the 1-day waiver.

## 5. Other scoring notes

- **Half-PPR (0.5/rec)** with 0.1/yd and 6-point receiving TDs. This nudges value toward RBs relative to full PPR and reduces the premium on high-volume, low-yardage slot receivers.
