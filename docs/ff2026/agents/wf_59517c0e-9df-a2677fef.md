## 4. QB run game theory: modeling the cascade

**Manager intel: "Once QBs go, they start to go."** This is the single most valuable input you have, and it is not in any corpus — it is private information about *this room*. Model it as a contagion: the per-pick hazard of a QB coming off the board jumps sharply after the first one goes.

### The snake structure — no corpus needed

For draft slot *s* in a 14-team snake:
- R1 = *s*, R2 = 29 − *s*, R3 = 28 + *s*, R4 = 57 − *s*, R5 = 56 + *s*, R6 = 85 − *s*
- Gaps alternate between **(29 − 2s)** and **(2s − 1)**, summing to 28.
- **Opponents who pick between your picks = gap − 1.**

| Slot | Long gap → opponents | Short gap → opponents |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27 → **26** | 1 → **0** |
| 4 | 21 → **20** | 7 → **6** |
| 7 | 15 → **14** | 13 → **12** |
| 8 | 13 → **12** | 15 → **14** |
| 11 | 7 → **6** | 21 → **20** |
| 14 | 1 → **0** | 27 → **26** |

Average across all slots: **13 opponents between your picks** (vs. 11 in a 12-team league — **18% more players removed per turn**).

### Cascade arithmetic

Let **q** = probability a given opponent takes a QB with a given pick, conditional on the run being live. **E[QBs gone while you wait] = opponents × q.**

| Opponents | q=0.15 (mild) | q=0.25 | q=0.35 (planning) | q=0.50 (violent) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 1.8 | 3.0 | **4.2** | 6.0 |
| 14 | 2.1 | 3.5 | **4.9** | 7.0 |
| 20 | 3.0 | 5.0 | **7.0** | 10.0 |
| **26** | 3.9 | 6.5 | **9.1** | **13.0** |

**Read the bottom row.** If you sit at slot 1 or slot 14 facing your 26-opponent gap and a run is live at q=0.35, **9.1 quarterbacks vanish before you pick again.** At q=0.50 — which is what "they start to go" describes — **13 vanish. The entire startable tier.** *(q is UNVERIFIED — it is a planning parameter, not a corpus figure. The conclusion holds across the whole range.)*

**Policy (a) — Start the run.** Take Allen at his ADP, picks 15–28, i.e. your Round 2.

- **What you give up:** the corpus prices that exact slot. `01:45–53` — "**Ashton Jeanty has an ADP of 10**... **Saquon Barkley 14**... **De'Von Achane 15**... **Omarion Hampton 15**... **Chase Brown 15**." `01:688` — "**Kenneth Walker's ADP is 22.**" `02:383` — "**Jeremiyah Love has an ADP of 26.4**." `02:1308` — "**Trey McBride (27.2) and Brock Bowers (27.6)**." Round 2 is where genuine difference-makers live. *(All CONFIRMED or SINGLE-SOURCE per the RB/TE blocks; note ADP figures for the same player differ by up to 5 picks across platforms — e.g. Chase Brown appears at 14.3, 15, and 17.8.)*
- **Gain:** Allen at ~8.0 PPG over a QB18 replacement (central model) vs. a mid-Round-2 RB at maybe 6–7 PPG over an RB36 replacement. **On raw VORP it is close, and that closeness is itself a product of the 14-team structure** — replacement is QB18, not QB14. But you only bank it once. And Allen at Round 2 costs ~3 more rounds than the Tier-2 QBs who trail him by only ~2–3 PPG.
- **The second-order benefit is a mirage.** Yes, you trigger the cascade and 13 rivals burn picks on QBs. But you cannot cash that in until your next pick, and what falls to you is the *flat* part of the board. If 3 extra rivals take QBs, you move up 3 spots in the WR queue — and the corpus says WR29 and WR31 both score 12.1 PPG. **Value of starting the run: ≈0.0–0.3 PPG. Negligible.**
- **Verdict: dominated. Do not pay Round 2 for a quarterback in a league where you get only 11 real players.**

**Policy (c) — Let it pass, take the best remaining.**

- One turn of waiting at 13 opponents and q=0.35: **4.6 QBs gone.** Two turns: **9.2 gone.** At q=0.50 and a long gap: **13 gone in a single wait.**
- If you pass with 5 QBs gone, you return with 9–11 gone. Pass again: 14+ gone. You are drafting QB15–18, which the corpus itself labels "double-digit rounds" material.
- **Cost: the full QB5 − QB18 gap. 46–91 season points (13 starts), 60–119 (17 starts).**
- **Verdict: catastrophic in this specific room. This is the policy the manager's intel exists to warn against.**

**Policy (b) — Move one pick before the expected run. ✅ RECOMMENDED.**

- Cost: a few picks of ADP reach, **not a full round.** You take your QB at the last pick before the cascade point rather than at his exact ADP.
- Benefit: you lock a top-6-to-8 QB at roughly market price and become **immune to the cascade** — after which every subsequent QB run is pure profit, because rivals burning picks on QBs push RB/WR down to you while you're already done.
- **Why it dominates:** it captures ~90% of policy (a)'s positional gain for ~20% of the cost, and it eliminates 100% of policy (c)'s tail risk. The asymmetry is the whole point — being one round *early* at QB costs 0–13 points (the flat flex curve); being one turn *late* costs 20–39.
- **Under uncertainty**, you cannot know when the run starts. That is what the trigger rule solves.

## 5. The in-draft trigger rule — usable in 90 seconds

### Derivation of N

You want to land **no worse than QB8**. So pull the trigger when:

> **N + (opponents × q) ≤ 8**, i.e. **N ≤ 8 − (opponents × q)**

At the planning value **q = 0.35**:

| Opponents before your next pick | N ≤ 8 − (opp × q) | **Trigger N** |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | 8 − 4.2 = 3.8 | **3** |
| 14 | 8 − 4.9 = 3.1 | **3** |
| 20 | 8 − 7.0 = 1.0 | **1** |
| 26 | 8 − 9.1 = **negative** | **0 — take pre-emptively** |

### THE CARD — read this at the table

> ## 🚨 QB TRIGGER
>
> **PRIMARY RULE: When the 3rd QB comes off the board, your VERY NEXT PICK is a QB.**
>
> **LONG-GAP OVERRIDE:** If you will not pick again for **15+ picks** — check this every single turn — then **N drops to 1.** One QB gone + a long gap + you're in Rounds 3–6 = **take yours now.**
>
> **PANIC FLOOR:** If **5 QBs are gone** and you don't have one, take the best QB available **immediately**, regardless of who else is on the board. You are already late.
>
> **HARD DEADLINE: you must own a QB by the end of Round 6 (pick 84). No exceptions.**
>
> **CEILING:** Do **not** take a QB before pick 29 (Round 3) — *unless Josh Allen is still there past pick 28*, in which case take him.

**Why N = 3 and not 2 or 4:** at 3 gone with an average 13-opponent gap, you expect to land QB4–QB8 — the top of the value window, at market price. At N = 4 you land QB5–QB9 and risk the cascade accelerating past you. At N = 2 you are reaching before the market moves and paying policy (a)'s price. **N = 3 is the point where the expected landing spot is still inside Tier 2 even if q turns out to be 0.50.**

**Why the hard deadline is Round 6 (pick 84):** the corpus's own 12-team ADP has 5 named QBs gone by pick 67 (Allen ~20, Jackson ~42, Maye ~52, Daniels ~62, Hurts 67), plus unnamed ones. Add two more managers competing for a fixed QB supply and expect **7–10 gone by pick 70 in a 14-team room** *(UNVERIFIED extrapolation)*. Entering Round 7 without a QB means shopping at QB10–13. ## 6. One QB or two? **One. And the reasoning changes what you draft.**

### Points-per-roster-slot

Roster = 13. K + D/ST = 2. Starters = 7. **Bench = 4.**

**What a rostered QB2 actually earns you.** It is *not* his points — it is his points **minus what a same-week waiver pickup would have scored**, because a QB2's only job is covering weeks your QB1 misses.

Corpus injury anchor, `02:1444`: "The average injury that causes a player to miss at least 1 game has a mean average length of **3.1 games missed**, with a typical NFL player expecting to be **healthy for about 14.2 games out of 16** per season." → **~1.8 games missed per player-season, all positions.** (SINGLE-SOURCE.)

And `02:1436`: "Compared to other positions in fantasy (**QB**, WR, TE), **running backs typically result in the most time missed**." → **QBs are below that average.** (SINGLE-SOURCE.)

**The arithmetic:**

```
Weeks QB2 would actually start
  = 1 (bye)  +  ~1.5 (expected injury weeks, QB < league avg of 1.8)
  ≈ 2.5 weeks

Marginal PPG of a drafted QB2 (≈QB15-16, ~14.0)
       over a same-week waiver QB (≈QB18-20, ~12.5)
  ≈ 1.5 PPG

MARGINAL SEASON VALUE OF THE QB2 ROSTER SLOT
  ≈ 2.5 weeks × 1.5 PPG ≈ 3.8 POINTS
```

**Under four points. Over an entire season. For 25% of your bench.**

**What the same slot earns as a flex-upside stash:** a 17-week option on a player who could become a weekly starter. The corpus is unambiguous that this is the correct use of a short bench:

- `02:1139` — "Rather than holding players just for depth, you can fill out your bench with **breakout candidates**, rather than handcuffs at running back or mid-tier, moderate-floor players."
- `02:1141` — "**If you cannot envision a scenario where someone can come in and make an immediate impact if given a starting opportunity, they go back to the waiver wire.**"

**A backup QB is, by definition, a player who cannot make an immediate impact — your QB slot is already occupied by a healthy starter.** He fails the corpus's own test on its face.

- `02:1133` — "Because the benches are smaller, **the waiver wire will be much bigger.** This means there will be solid players every week to pick up if you are in a bind or it's a bye week." (Your league's own math confirms it: 182 rostered here vs. 192 in a 12-team/16-spot league.)

### The one honest argument *for* a QB2 — and why it still loses

Your waiver order **resets weekly by inverse standings**. If you are in first place, you have **priority 14 of 14, every single week.** When your QB goes down in Week 9, you cannot outbid anyone — there is no FAAB to spend.

**But this bites far harder at RB than at QB.** In a week where a starting RB's backup becomes a league-winner, all 13 rivals want him and you get nothing. In a week where you need a QB, **most rivals already have one and will not use a claim on QB15.** You will very likely still get the top available quarterback. The inverse-standings penalty is real but is mostly a *running back* problem.

### Verdict, and the non-obvious corollary

> **ROSTER EXACTLY ONE QUARTERBACK. Use all 4 bench slots on RB/WR upside.**
>
> **COROLLARY — and this is the part that should change your draft:** because you are carrying only one, and because a weekly-resetting inverse-standings waiver order means *winning makes your replacement options worse*, **the insurance you would normally buy with a bench QB must instead be bought with draft capital at QB1.**

That is the thread tying every section together. It means: **do not punt to a quarterback with a shaky job or a shaky body.** It is a direct argument for the Tier-2 window over a Round-8 dart, and it is why Allen's corpus-confirmed "hasn't missed a start in five seasons" is a genuine asset rather than trivia — even though I still won't pay Round 2 for it.

Two health flags the corpus raises that bear directly on this: **Mahomes** is "returning from a knee injury that ended his 2025 regular season" (`01:2183`), and **Daniels'** "second pro season was limited by injuries as he played in only seven games" (`01:2667`). Both SINGLE-SOURCE. Neither is disqualifying; both are reasons not to make one of them your *only* quarterback without a second thought.

**One conditional exception:** if the trigger rule fails you and you end Round 7 without a QB, the position has become high-variance and a second late QB becomes a defensible hedge. But by then you have already taken the loss. Prevention beats insurance.
