## 3. Where the sources genuinely disagree (this is where you win picks)

**1.01 — Gibbs vs. Bijan.** ESPN ADP, Mike Clay, 4for4, FFC and TWSN all have **Gibbs #1** (Detroit ranks 3rd in 2026 implied team totals per First Down Studio; Atlanta ranks **25th** — that's the whole argument). **Field Yates and Sleeper ADP (1.2) have Bijan #1.** Practical: either is defensible; the tiebreaker is offensive environment, and it favors Gibbs.

**WR1 order — Chase vs. Nacua.** FFC half-PPR has **Nacua 2.5, Chase 3.4** (Nacua ahead). CBS, Yates, and "the market slots him third across basically every platform" have **Chase ahead**. ESPN's default has Nacua as its top WR. This is a genuine coin-flip; Nacua is a *valid 1.01/1.02 consideration* per NFL.com.

**The single biggest exploitable split: elite TEs.** CBS ADP has **McBride 27.2 / Bowers 27.6** (late R2/early R3). FFC public-mock half-PPR has **McBride 44 / Bowers 47** (R4). 4for4 has **Bowers 21st overall**. That is a **20-pick, two-round disagreement.** In an ESPN league drafting off ESPN defaults, expect the TEs to fall closer to the FFC number than the CBS number.

**De'Von Achane.** ESPN default = **10th overall.** Footballguys = **RB11.** Another market source = **15.0.** RotoWire explicitly flags this: "De'Von Achane (10) is ahead of Ashton Jeanty (12) in ESPN's default ranks, while Jeanty is a few spots ahead of Achane pretty much everywhere else." The Miami reset (no Tua, no McDaniel) is the reason non-ESPN sources are lower.

**Jeremiyah Love.** ESPN default **15** · Sleeper **15.2** · Underdog **23** · NFFC **25** · FFC (8/4) **29**. RotoWire: Love at 15 on ESPN "is listed a round-plus above industry consensus." Yahoo ran a piece titled "Why Jeremiyah Love is not worth the reach," and CBS's RB Tiers 2.0 headline is literally "**Jeremiyah Love, Breece Hall fall**." Add a high ankle sprain. In an ESPN room, Love is the most overpriced player on the default board.

**Nico Collins.** Mike Clay has him **WR9**; Field Yates has him **#40 / WR15**. ~19 picks of analyst disagreement.

**Christian McCaffrey.** Dave Richard's RB Tiers 2.0 *returns CMC to Tier 1*. Heath Cummings would take **James Cook and Chase Brown ahead of him**. CBS separately ran "Christian McCaffrey won you your leagues in 2025 but avoiding him in 2026 might be your only path back" — 30 years old, coming off a **450-touch** season, and he was **out of practice with tightness** in the last week.

**ESPN defaults are the softest board in the industry.** RotoWire, verbatim: "Among major redraft platforms, ESPN has the most easily exploitable default ranks, with large discrepancies between the listed order of players in an ESPN draft room and the fantasy football ADPs seen on other sites." **You are in an ESPN league. This is your single largest edge today.**

## 4. Tiers and cliffs, Rounds 1–6

Pick-number mapping so you can use this the moment your slot is randomized at ~1:00 PM:
- **12-team:** R1 = 1–12 · R2 = 13–24 · R3 = 25–36 · R4 = 37–48 · R5 = 49–60 · R6 = 61–72
- **10-team:** R1 = 1–10 · R2 = 11–20 · R3 = 21–30 · R4 = 31–40 · R5 = 41–50 · R6 = 51–60

**TIER 1 — the true elite (picks 1–5). CLIFF AFTER PICK 5.**
Gibbs, Bijan, Chase, Nacua, JSN. Multiple sources explicitly say "there are **five Tier 1 players** entering the 2026 fantasy season" and name exactly these. If you pick 1–5 you get one; at 6+ you do not. This is the sharpest cliff on the board.

**TIER 2 — the RB anchor block (picks 6–12). CLIFF AFTER ~PICK 12.**
McCaffrey, Jonathan Taylor, Jeanty, James Cook, Achane, Hampton (+ Kyren Williams on Sleeper). All carry a red flag: TWSN — "three of these running backs have a few red flags that prevent them from being elite: **Christian McCaffrey, De'Von Achane and Jonathan Taylor**." Draft Sharks: "the top 10–11 RBs should all be off the board by **picks 15 to 20**"; STACKED: "the top 12 RBs are expected to be gone by the **end of Round 2**." **In a 10-team league this cliff lands mid-R2; in a 12-team it lands at the 1/2 turn.**

**TIER 3 — WR1 block + RB projection block (picks 13–24). This is the fattest, safest tier on the board.**
WRs: Lamb (14.3–14.7), Jefferson (15.7–18.5), ARSB (16.8), London (17.3), then Rice (22.5) / Pickens (22.7) / A.J. Brown (23.4).
RBs: Saquon (13.4–15), Chase Brown (14.3–17.8), Love (15–29), Henry, Walker (22.9).
Key structural note from TWSN: Hampton and Walker are a **projection tier** — "don't have that RB1 job locked up, but there's a clear path." Karabell-style read: the WRs in this band are the better risk-adjusted buy, and multiple sources say "RBs in this range are generally **overpriced** in relation to the WRs." **In half-PPR that RB-over-WR overpricing is less wrong than it would be in full PPR — but it's still an overpricing.**

**TIER 4 — the elite-TE window + WR2 block (picks 25–40). CLIFF AFTER BOWERS/McBRIDE.**
McBride (27.2) and Bowers (27.6) go back-to-back, then **nothing at TE until Loveland at 46.7 — a ~19-pick vacuum.** CBS: "managers willing to take the chance on McBride and Bowers by the back end of Round 2, and on Loveland and Warren by the end of Round 5." Around them: Nico Collins (26.2), Nabers (27.4), Judkins, Breece Hall (32), Egbuka (33–43), Garrett Wilson (35), McMillan (34), Javonte Williams (35), Lamar (39).

**TIER 5 — the WR3/RB3 morass (picks 41–60). No cliff, a long flat plain.**
McConkey (45–52), Loveland (46.7), Maye (50–54), McLaurin (51.4), Waddle (53.2), Irving (54.5), Henderson (~54), Warren (55.9), Burden (56.9), Higgins (58), Daniels (~63), D.J. Moore (63.5). This is where **half-PPR scoring should change your order** (see §6) and where the ESPN-default edge is fattest.

**TIER 6 — the second TE cliff and the QB dead zone (picks 61–80).**
After Warren (55.9) there's another gap to **LaPorta 70.4 / Pitts 71.1 / Kraft 76.3** — CBS calls this a genuine three-way tie for TE5. At QB: after Hurts (67) you fall off into the Mahomes/Burrow/Nix/Caleb Williams pack, with **Justin Herbert at QB15 in the double-digit rounds.**

**The two cliffs that matter most for YOUR roster:** with only **4 bench spots** and **1 QB / 1 TE / 1 K / 1 D-ST** required, you can afford to let the elite-TE cliff pass and the QB cliff pass, then take the *last* member of each falling tier. What you cannot afford is missing the RB cliff at pick ~12–20, because a 13-man roster with 2 RB + 1 FLEX gives you almost no room to stream out of an RB hole.

## 6. Half-PPR vs. full-PPR — what changes in YOUR 0.5-PPR league

| Player | Half-PPR ADP | Full-PPR ADP | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trey McBride (TE, ARI) | **44** | 37 | **−7 in half** |
| Brock Bowers (TE, LV) | **47** | 41 | **−6 in half** |
| Ladd McConkey (WR, LAC) | **50** | 45 | **−5 in half** |

**Read the pattern and extend it.** Reception-volume archetypes are worth ~4–7 picks less to you than the full-PPR internet is pricing them:
- **Fade a half-round:** Trey McBride (126 catches in 2025 — the TE reception record — is the most reception-levered profile in football), Brock Bowers, Ladd McConkey, Chase Brown, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Josh Downs, Chris Godwin, Khalil Shakir-types, and pass-catching committee RBs generally.
- **Bump a half-round:** TD-and-yardage archetypes — **Derrick Henry** (top-7 RB at 32, still), **Kenneth Walker III** (KC red-zone volume), **Bijan Robinson**, **Ashton Jeanty**, **Javonte Williams**, plus big-play/deep WRs (**A.J. ## 8. Applying this to your specific league

- **13 active roster spots, 4 bench, 9 starters.** 12 teams × 13 = **156 players drafted**; 10 teams × 13 = **130**. So the whole draft is ~11–13 rounds. Your top-60 board covers R1–R5 (12-team) or R1–R6 (10-team).
- **4 bench spots kills handcuffing.** You cannot roster Gibbs + a Gibbs handcuff + a Judkins handcuff. National late-round ADP for backups will *not* hold in your room — expect handcuffs and dart throws to fall much further than ADP.
- **Waiver order resets weekly by inverse standings and there's no FAAB and no acquisition limit.** That further devalues bench hoarding: you can just re-acquire. Draft for starting-lineup ceiling, not depth.
- **Only 4 playoff teams and 2-week playoff matchups**, seeded by total points. That rewards the highest-ceiling roster, not the safest — which argues for the elite Tier 1 pick and for taking the high-variance side of any coin flip.
- **You are in an ESPN room.** RotoWire's explicit finding is that ESPN's default board is the most exploitable in the industry, with **Achane (10), Jeanty (12), and Jeremiyah Love (15)** named as the specific distortions. Love at 15 with a high ankle sprain is the clearest fade; Jeanty at 12 on ESPN is the clearest buy relative to his 9–10.8 market ADP.
- **Half-PPR + only 1 TE required + the McBride/Bowers half-PPR discount** means the disciplined play is to skip the 27-ADP elite TE, let the ~19-pick TE vacuum pass, and take Loveland (46.7) or Warren (55.9) — or, if the room is ESPN-default-driven and the TEs slide toward their FFC prices (44/47), pounce there instead.
