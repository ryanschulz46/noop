Decision rules

QB — the trigger, not the round

Your league runs on QBs, and 14 teams need 14 starters. Waiting is safe in a 12-team league because QB13 is startable; here QB15+ is not. Don't pick a round — pick a trigger.

Rule: once 6 QBs are gone, you take yours within your next two picks. That leaves 8 startable arms for 8 remaining teams — after that you are drafting into the cliff, and the run is exactly what makes it collapse fast. Roster one QB. A second costs 25% of your bench for a bye you can stream around.

TE — pay up or punt, don't shop the middle

Replacement is ~TE15-16 rather than TE13, which widens the elite-TE edge relative to a 12-team league even after half-PPR takes ~2.5 PPG off a reception-heavy tight end. The middle tier is where you pay a real pick for a marginal edge.

Either take a top-3 TE at market price, or wait and take your TE in the last third of the draft. Avoid the TE6-TE10 range entirely.

D/ST — one round earlier than instinct

The scoring is standard, but the tail is real: −7 at 550+ yards allowed and −5 at 46+ points means a bad defense posts genuinely negative weeks, not merely quiet ones. With 14 rostered, the streaming pool is thin.

Take your D/ST in round 12, not 13, and take a unit with a good early schedule.

Bench — no handcuffs, no stashes

Four bench spots is 56 player-weeks for the whole season. A speculative stash that pays off in Week 10 is worth a fraction of a Week-1 contributor when only 4 of 14 teams advance. Every bench player should be someone you would consider starting in the next three weeks.

Kicker last, always. And never draft a second kicker or a third TE.
